# Product Vision & Market Research Plan
## Pakistan Citizen Assistant

**Working product name:** Pakistan Citizen Assistant  
**Working tagline:** Your Voice. Your Rights. One Assistant.  
**Repository:** RYK Urdu Voice Complaint Routing System  
**Document status:** Product vision proposal — subject to user research and validation  
**Last updated:** 23 November 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Pakistan Citizen Assistant is a proposed AI-powered, multilingual citizen-support platform designed to make public-service complaint processes easier to understand and access.

A citizen should be able to explain a problem in everyday language, preferably by speaking in Urdu or English. The assistant will ask simple follow-up questions, understand the user's location and the nature of the issue, identify a relevant verified authority, prepare a clear complaint draft, and guide the citizen through the appropriate official submission process.

The product is intended for ordinary citizens, including people with limited literacy or digital skills. Typing must not be compulsory. Voice input, simple language, large controls, and guided choices should make the experience approachable.

The long-term vision is a Pakistan-wide platform covering relevant federal, provincial, district, and local public-service authorities. The initial university FYP remains a focused prototype and will not claim nationwide operational coverage.

## 2. Problem Statement

Citizens may face several barriers when trying to raise a public-service issue:

- They may not know which department or authority is responsible.
- Official forms and complaint procedures can be difficult to understand.
- Writing a formal complaint may be challenging.
- A person may have difficulty typing, navigating websites, or using English-language portals.
- The correct authority can depend on province, district, service provider, and issue type.
- Submission and tracking processes vary between authorities.

The platform aims to reduce these barriers through guided, accessible assistance. It cannot guarantee that an authority will accept, investigate, or resolve a complaint.

## 3. Product Vision

**Vision:** Make it easier for every person in Pakistan to understand where and how to raise a public-service concern, using a language and interaction method they can comfortably use.

**Mission:** Turn a citizen's explanation into a clear, user-approved complaint and guide them to the correct, verified official channel.

**Core promise:** Explain your problem in your own words. Get guided help to reach the relevant authority.

## 4. Target Users

The product is intended to support:

- Citizens with limited literacy or digital skills.
- People who are more comfortable speaking than typing.
- Students and university communities.
- Women, older adults, and people with disabilities.
- Residents of rural and urban communities.
- Citizens who need help understanding public-service procedures.
- Overseas Pakistanis, where the relevant service and jurisdiction permit their use.

These are intended user groups, not claims that the product has already been tested with them. User interviews and accessibility testing are required.

## 5. Core User Experience

1. **Choose a language:** Start with Urdu and English. Add other languages after validation.
2. **Explain the issue:** Speak, type, or select a simple category. Voice is optional.
3. **Answer guided questions:** The assistant asks one short question at a time to collect missing details.
4. **Confirm location:** Ask only for the location detail needed to identify the responsible jurisdiction.
5. **Identify the authority:** Suggest a department or service provider using a maintained, verified directory.
6. **Prepare the complaint:** Draft a clear complaint using only information supplied or confirmed by the user.
7. **Attach evidence if relevant:** Allow optional documents, images, or reference numbers, with clear privacy guidance.
8. **Review and approve:** Show the complete complaint and destination. Obtain explicit user approval before any submission.
9. **Submit or guide:** Use an authorized official integration where available. Otherwise, guide the user to the official channel or provide a ready-to-use draft.
10. **Track honestly:** Save the user's reference number and show status only when confirmed by an official response or verified integration.

## 6. Proposed Feature Set

### Citizen-facing features

- Urdu and English interface.
- Conversational AI assistant.
- Optional speech-to-text and voice guidance.
- Text input and simple selectable answers.
- Location-aware authority suggestions.
- AI-assisted complaint drafting.
- Missing-information checks.
- User review, correction, and explicit approval.
- Optional evidence attachment.
- Official portal and contact directory.
- Complaint history and reference-number storage.
- Status tracking where an official source supports it.
- Clear instructions when online submission is unavailable.
- Accessibility support: readable text, large buttons, simple labels, and low-bandwidth design.

### Administrative features

- Manage department and authority directory entries.
- Review and update official links and contact details.
- Configure jurisdiction and complaint categories.
- Maintain source and verification dates.
- Manage authorized staff access.
- Review system quality and routing errors using privacy-conscious aggregate metrics.
- Maintain audit logs for administrative actions.

## 7. Public-Service Coverage Model

The national vision is broad coverage of relevant public services. Coverage must be built as a verified directory, not as an unverified static list.

Initial service-sector groups to research:

1. Police and public safety.
2. Health, hospitals, and public health services.
3. Education, schools, colleges, and universities.
4. Electricity and power distribution.
5. Gas services.
6. Water supply, sanitation, sewerage, and waste management.
7. Traffic, roads, and road safety.
8. Public transport and railways.
9. Consumer protection and product/service complaints.
10. Social welfare and public assistance.
11. Land, property records, and revenue services.
12. Telecommunications and internet services.
13. Taxation and government revenue services.
14. Agriculture, livestock, and farmer support.
15. Environment, pollution, and conservation.
16. Local government and municipal administration.
17. Labour, employment, and workplace-related public services.
18. Women and child protection services.
19. Food safety and public food standards.
20. Disaster management and relief services.
21. Identity, civil registration, and passport-related public services.
22. Housing and development authorities.
23. Postal and other public utility services.

This is an initial research taxonomy, not a complete or officially approved list. The responsible authority may differ by province, district, locality, provider, and complaint type. Legal, emergency, and regulated matters require careful routing and clear disclaimers.

### Required directory fields

Each authority record should include:

- Official authority or department name.
- Government level: federal, provincial, district, or local; or public/private service provider where relevant.
- Province, district, city, and service jurisdiction.
- Supported issue categories.
- Official website and complaint portal.
- Official contact details, where published.
- Submission method and required documents.
- Whether an authorized technical integration exists.
- Source URL and date last verified.
- Directory review status and responsible maintainer.

The assistant must not invent department names, phone numbers, links, jurisdiction rules, deadlines, or submission requirements.

## 8. Differentiation and Value Proposition

The proposed differentiator is not simply an online complaint form. It is a guided, language-accessible assistant that helps a person move from an everyday explanation to the correct official process.

| Common user barrier | Proposed product response |
|---|---|
| Does not know the responsible department | Ask about the issue and location, then suggest a verified authority |
| Finds formal forms difficult | Ask short, guided questions |
| Has difficulty typing | Offer optional voice input and selectable answers |
| Cannot write a formal complaint | Prepare a draft from user-confirmed facts |
| Is unsure where to submit | Provide a verified official channel and clear instructions |
| Needs to follow up | Save reference details and show verified status when available |

The product must complement existing official complaint channels rather than claim to replace them.

## 9. Trust, Privacy, and Safety Principles

- Never invent facts, evidence, allegations, identities, or legal claims.
- Ask the user to confirm uncertain or missing information.
- Obtain explicit consent before submitting or sharing complaint information.
- Collect only information necessary for the specific process.
- Explain why personal information or evidence is requested.
- Protect personal data with appropriate authentication, encryption, access controls, and secure storage.
- Do not sell citizens' complaint data or expose it for advertising.
- Keep access to evidence restricted and define retention/deletion rules.
- Clearly distinguish a draft, a portal hand-off, a successful submission, and an authority-confirmed status.
- Never claim a complaint has been submitted unless the official integration confirms it.
- Do not fabricate tracking updates or resolution outcomes.
- For immediate danger or medical emergencies, direct users to appropriate emergency services; the app is not an emergency-response service.
- Legal information must be presented as general guidance, not a substitute for qualified legal advice.

## 10. Existing Alternatives and Market Validation

Pakistan already has official and private channels for different complaint types. The product must research these channels before deciding where it adds value.

A known national comparator is Pakistan Citizen's Portal. Other complaint channels may be operated by provincial governments, regulators, utilities, local authorities, and service providers.

The product should not claim to be the first complaint app, the only national platform, or guaranteed to resolve complaints.

### Research questions

- Which existing channels do citizens know and use?
- Where do users get confused or abandon the process?
- Which languages and interaction methods do users prefer?
- Which complaint categories are most frequent in the selected pilot area?
- Which authorities provide usable official submission and tracking channels?
- Would citizens trust an AI assistant to prepare a complaint if they can review and approve it?
- What accessibility and connectivity barriers exist?

### Validation plan

- Interview citizens from different age groups and education levels.
- Interview university students and staff for the FYP pilot.
- Speak with relevant public-service staff or subject experts where possible.
- Review official complaint procedures and portal requirements.
- Test prototypes with real users using consent and non-sensitive scenarios.
- Record task completion, routing accuracy, user comprehension, and satisfaction.
- Revise the product based on evidence rather than assumptions.

## 11. Success Measures

Possible pilot metrics:

- Percentage of users who can complete the guided intake.
- Correct-authority suggestion rate, reviewed by a human evaluator.
- Complaint draft accuracy and percentage of unsupported details (target: zero).
- Percentage of users who understand the submission destination.
- Time required to prepare a complaint draft.
- User-reported ease of use and trust.
- Accessibility task completion across different user groups.
- Percentage of directory entries with current, verified sources.
- Successful submission confirmation rate, measured only where integrations exist.

Downloads and registrations alone do not demonstrate public value. Meaningful usage, accuracy, trust, and successful navigation of official processes matter more.

## 12. Sustainability Model

The citizen-facing core should remain free wherever possible, especially for people with limited resources.

Potential future sustainability options, subject to partnerships and legal review:

- Institutional licensing for universities and organizations.
- Authorized service-management dashboards for partner institutions.
- Government or NGO partnerships.
- Grants and public-interest digital inclusion programs.
- Clearly labelled public-awareness sponsorships that do not influence complaint routing.

Personal complaint data must never be sold. Any analytics must be privacy-preserving and based on a legitimate purpose.

## 13. Delivery Roadmap

### Phase 1 — University FYP prototype

The original FYP scope remains the first implementation target:

- University complaints.
- Hostel complaints.
- Transport complaints.
- Urdu and English guided intake.
- Optional voice input and speech-to-text.
- Complaint classification and suggested routing.
- AI-assisted complaint draft.
- User review and approval.
- Basic admin dashboard and complaint records.
- Basic status workflow for prototype records, clearly labelled as simulated unless connected to a real authority.

The university name, actual departments, and institutional complaint process must be confirmed before real routing is configured.

### Phase 2 — Local pilot

- Select a defined district or service area.
- Research a limited set of relevant authorities.
- Verify official submission processes and directory entries.
- Test with citizens and community organizations.
- Add authorized integrations only after permission and technical feasibility checks.

### Phase 3 — Provincial expansion

- Expand verified directories across provinces and territories.
- Add jurisdiction-specific workflows.
- Improve language support and accessibility.
- Establish partnerships and operational maintenance processes.

### Phase 4 — Pakistan-wide platform

- Broader federal, provincial, district, and local coverage.
- Mobile and web applications.
- More regional languages.
- Scalable security, monitoring, support, and directory governance.
- Official integrations where authorized and available.

National coverage is a long-term goal, not a claim about current prototype capability.

## 14. Proposed Technical Direction

The following is a candidate architecture to validate during implementation:

- **Mobile client:** React Native or another suitable cross-platform framework.
- **Backend/API:** Python with FastAPI.
- **Database:** PostgreSQL.
- **Speech processing:** Urdu/English speech-to-text model selected after testing.
- **AI layer:** Language model with structured extraction, classification, and controlled complaint drafting.
- **Admin dashboard:** Web application.
- **Evidence storage:** Private object storage with access controls.
- **Authentication:** Secure account and session management.
- **Integrations:** Official APIs or authorized portal workflows only.

Technology choices may change after prototype testing, cost assessment, and review of available services. No integration should be represented as operational until it has been implemented and tested.

## 15. Open Decisions

The following require confirmation before implementation:

- Final product name and branding.
- Mobile-first, web-first, or simultaneous mobile and web launch strategy.
- University name and approved department mapping for Phase 1.
- Exact FYP evaluation requirements and available development time.
- Selected AI and speech services after accuracy and cost tests.
- Data hosting, privacy, and retention requirements.
- Which official channels permit integrations or automated submission.

## 16. Product Positioning Statement

**Pakistan Citizen Assistant is a proposed AI-powered citizen-support platform that helps people explain public-service problems in simple language, prepare user-approved complaints, and find verified official channels — with a long-term goal of accessible coverage across Pakistan.**
